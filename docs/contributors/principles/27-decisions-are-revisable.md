# §27 — A decision is the best answer on what was known; a better way found later is raised at once

**The owner decides on the knowledge and experience available at the time, and some of those
decisions will turn out wrong.** The owner, 2026-10-29, after D373 overturned D371 and D372's "the
group stands for its leader's stall" a day after they were decided: *"The decisions I make are based
on the information and experiences I have available at the time. Later on as I gain new knowledge
and experiences, some of them will invariably turn out to be bad decisions, to be rescinded or
changed."* And: *"Our dev cycle will invariably turn up new and better ways to do things, so I want
to know whenever you discover any."*

So a decision in the log is a starting point, not a wall:

- **Say so as soon as you see it.** When a device test, a log, research or the code shows a decided
  rule hurting the experience, or a better way to do what it does, tell the owner then, citing the
  decision and the evidence, with the change you propose. Don't wait for the owner to notice, and
  don't save it for a report at the end.
- **Never defend a decision because it is recorded**, and never refuse or bend work around one that
  the evidence says is wrong. The decision is the owner's to change (AGENTS.md rule 1); raising it is
  everyone's job.
- **A change is a new decision that names what it amends** (as D373 amends D372 §5), so the log keeps
  why each rule was made and why it was changed. The old entry is history and is not rewritten.
- **The same holds for the process**: a better way to test, gate, brief or deploy is proposed
  (§20 for gates), not quietly adopted or quietly forgone.

**Bites when:** you build around a rule that a test just showed is wrong, explain a bad result by
"that is what D-something says", or mention a better approach only in passing.
