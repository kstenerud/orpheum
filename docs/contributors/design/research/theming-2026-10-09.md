## 1. The question, made precise

D138 §4 argued from the size of Jellyfin's theme community that a theming seam *"exists either
way"*, so it is better to ship a small, declared one than suffer an accidental one. The owner's
experience is the other half of the same evidence: the themes exist, and they rot. So the
question is three questions:

1. How much do people actually want and use themes, measured against what else they ask for?
2. When an app changes, what happens to its themes, and how fast?
3. Which jobs do themes do, and is a third-party theme the right tool for each, or is a built-in
   setting?

Then: what a public seam would cost Orpheum, and what lies between "a seam" and "nothing".

## 2. Demand

### 2.1 Jellyfin: a large theme community, a small number of requests

- **Stars [M].** A GitHub search for `jellyfin theme` returns **405** repositories (383 on
  2026-10-05). The top themes now: `ElegantFin` 2,193, `JellySkin` 1,027, `Ultrachromic` 999,
  `abyss-jellyfin` 751, `scyfin` 738, `ZestyTheme` 533, `finimalism` 461, `Jellyfish` 309,
  `GlassFin` 245, `zombie-release` 186. For scale, the server, `jellyfin/jellyfin`, has
  **57,923** stars and the web client, `jellyfin-web`, **3,909**: the most-starred theme has
  more than half the web client's stars.
- **Requests [M].** Jellyfin's feature board
  ([features.jellyfin.org](https://features.jellyfin.org/), Fider API, `most-wanted`): the
  hundredth request has **89** votes; the top ones are offline mode on Android (1,820), removing
  an item from Continue Watching (1,727) and a watchlist (1,295). **No request about how the
  client looks reaches the top hundred.** The largest visual ones are *"Set Theme Globally"*
  (46, [post 755](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/755)), *"Allow more customization of
  Jellyfin Web Branding"* (15), *"Make it so themes are not hard coded"* (11), *"Add way to
  upload user themes"* (10, [post 145](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/145)), *"Themes/Skins
  or clone Plex layout"* (10, declined), *"Custom User CSS"* (7, completed), *"Automatically
  switch theme from light to dark"* (5), *"Black theme"* (2). Theme *songs* outdraw visual
  themes on the same board: *"Volume setting for Theme Songs"* has 103.
- **Why people ask [user].** The declined *"Themes/Skins or clone Plex layout"*
  ([post 2509](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/2509)): *"People prefer plex because of the
  layout and organizing of the titles … that's what keeping me from doing a hard switch over to
  jellyfin."* *"TV Customization + Default themes"* ([post
  1937](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/1937)): *"the most popular ones are the ones
  resembling Netflix or Disney+. It would be cool to have a couple of themes like that installed
  by default."* Both ask for a **better default**, not for a seam. The maintainers' answer to the
  second is in §3.1.

*Reading it (judgement):* the stars measure a community of a few thousand enthusiasts who
bookmark CSS, which is real, but the votes say that among Jellyfin's users the look is a minor
want beside features. The two requests with reasons both treat theming as a route to a better
default look, which is what D245 already commits Orpheum to build.

### 2.2 Plex: no user themes for five years, and almost no one asks

- **Plex shipped built-in colour themes in November 2021** [doc]
  ([Plex blog, *Finding Your True Colors*](https://www.plex.tv/blog/finding-your-true-colors-with-plex/)):
  Default/Dark, Light, **High Contrast** (*"Dark default theme with true blacks"*, *"for
  accessibility purposes"*), and two accent themes, Bubble Gum and Moonlight, for paying
  subscribers only. They are values of design tokens (`accentBackground`, `focusBackground`),
  and the post ends: *"(Sorry, right now we don't support user-created themes… yet)."* Nearly
  five years later there still are none.
- **The forum [M]**, Discourse search on titles: the 2021 announcement drew one reply
  (*"Add more please and nice work"*, [topic 761417](https://forums.plex.tv/t/761417));
  *"Light theme is needed"* (2023, 0 likes, [857647](https://forums.plex.tv/t/857647)); *"Make
  the interface customizable for ANY need with CSS"* (2025-10-04, 1 like,
  [932114](https://forums.plex.tv/t/932114)), written during the backlash to the new apps,
  proposing CSS as a fix for layout. Every other "theme" hit is about theme *music*.
- Against this, [D138](../../decisions/working-notes.md) §1 found **51% of likes on Plex's
  fifty most-liked topics** are about the interface, and coded them as focus behaviour, rollback,
  lost features, missing customisation and click depth, *"not typography, not motion, not
  colour"*. The customisation people fight for at Plex is the arrangement of the home screen and
  sidebar, which D138 §2 already made user state.
- **The one third-party Plex theme of note** is `theme.park` (3,103 stars [M]), themes for
  fifty self-hosted apps injected through a reverse proxy. Its Plex stylesheet has had one
  change since May 2022 (line endings, 2023) [M], and its tracker holds *"Plex theme broken after
  Plex update"* ([#291](https://github.com/themepark-dev/theme.park/issues/291), 2021).

### 2.3 Emby, Kodi, and the apps outside the field

- **Emby** ships built-in themes and a server-wide custom-CSS field (UNVERIFIED in 2026: its
  forum returned 403). Its theme community is small [M]: GitHub search for `emby theme css`
  returns 5 repositories, the largest 51 stars and last pushed in 2018.
- **Kodi** is the field's real skinning platform: skins replace layout, not only colour. Its
  default, Estuary, ships **thirteen built-in colour palettes** beside its default (`brown`,
  `charcoal`, … `violet`, read from `xbmc/addons/skin.estuary/colors` [M]), maintained with the
  app. The Hacker News comment already quoted in ui-design-practice §4.2 is the owner's
  experience in a stranger's words: *"I always end up going back to the default Kodi theme -
  it's ugly but it works."*
- **Home Assistant**: HACS's default list holds **107 themes** beside 772 frontend plugins and
  3,262 integrations [M] (`hacs/default`, 2026-10-09).
- **Obsidian**: **846** community themes against 8,599 plugins [M] (`obsidian-releases`). The
  theme count doubled in nine months (414 on 2026-01-01).
- **VS Code**: **18,606** extensions in the Themes category [M] (Marketplace query); the
  most-installed colour themes are GitHub Theme (20.3 M installs), One Dark Pro (12.8 M) and
  Dracula (11.0 M).

*Reading it (judgement):* themes thrive where the product is a tool a person sits in for hours
and the seam is narrow and declared (VS Code, Obsidian, Home Assistant's dashboards). In media
servers they thrive only at Jellyfin, whose default look is the field's most criticised, and are
nearly absent where the default is liked (Plex's complaints are about structure, not colour) or
where built-in palettes exist (Kodi's Estuary, Plex's 2021 themes).

## 3. Maintenance and breakage

### 3.1 Jellyfin 12.0, one month on

Jellyfin 12.0 shipped on 2026-09-08 [doc]
([release](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/releases/tag/v12.0)) with a new default layout
(Modern, built on MUI) and this line under *Layout and themes*: *"All themes now derive from a
shared base theme built on CSS variables … **Custom themes may need to be adjusted**."* The
release candidates had run since 2026-06-21, eleven weeks.

**The fifteen most-starred Jellyfin themes, on 2026-10-09** [M] (clones, commit logs, READMEs and
issues; stars from the search above):

| Status for 12.x | Themes | Stars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Updated for the new default layout | `Ultrachromic` (fixes 9 Sep–2 Oct; its *"now broken"* issue still open), `abyss-jellyfin` (*"styles both the Modern React/MUI interface and the Desktop (Legacy) interface"*), `scyfin` (fixes 10 Sep–7 Oct), `Jellyfish` | 2,797 (35%) |
| Works only if the user switches back to the Legacy layout | `ElegantFin` (*"On Jellyfin v12, you will need to set … Display Mode to 'Desktop (Legacy)' … The new (non-legacy) UI is not yet fully supported"*), `catppuccin/jellyfin` (*"select the 'TV' or one of the 'Legacy' display modes"*) | 2,323 (29%) |
| Work in progress | `GlassFin` (*"Getting there slowly"*), `Flow` (*"12.x/modern layout Compatibility (WIP)"*) | 362 (5%) |
| No update since 12.0 | `JellySkin` (last commit 2024-07-01), `ZestyTheme` (2025-11-07), `finimalism` (2026-02-16; its 12.0 issue lists *"quite a few visual and layout issues"*), `zombie-release` (*"for Jellyfin 10.9.x"*), `Monochromic` (2021), `NeutralFin`, `jellyfin-theme` | 2,561 (32%) |

So a month after a release with eleven weeks' notice, **four of the fifteen** (35% of their
stars) work with the default the release ships. On 2026-09-10 one user opened the same issue,
*"Jellyfin 12 is out! And it seems like the theme is now broken when upgrading"*, on **eight**
of these repositories (e.g. [Ultrachromic
#144](https://github.com/CTalvio/Ultrachromic/issues/144), [abyss
#100](https://github.com/AumGupta/abyss-jellyfin/issues/100), 22 reactions). Across the fourteen
repositories searched, **26 of 108 issues** opened since the first release candidate name version 12,
the Modern layout, or breakage [M].

**The package manager is broken too.** `jellyfin-plugin-skin-manager`, the *"strongest single
signal"* of D138 §4, has had no code change since November 2024 [M]; its open issues include
*"All themes have issues since 10.11.0"* ([#85](https://github.com/danieladov/jellyfin-plugin-skin-manager/issues/85),
October 2025) and *"Plugin needs Jellyfin 12.0 update"*
([#92](https://github.com/danieladov/jellyfin-plugin-skin-manager/issues/92), 14 reactions).

**The maintainers' own view [user].** Declining built-in copies of popular themes
([post 1937](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/1937)): *"a lot of these themes do introduce
issues by not supporting mobile devices, TVs, or breaking the ui in certain other edge cases."*
Jellyfin's documentation [doc]
([CSS Customization](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/clients/css-customization/)): *"Support is
not provided for issues caused by custom CSS."* Declining a theming rewrite ([post
2616](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/2616)): *"A custom cross platform theming solution
would be a project in itself."*

### 3.2 Hashed class names do not stop themers; they make the breakage certain

Jellyfin 12's Modern layout is MUI, whose styles are generated with hashed class names. MUI's
documentation is plain [doc]
([How to customize](https://mui.com/material-ui/customization/how-to-customize/)): the generated
names *"can't be used as CSS selectors because they are unstable"*; target `.MuiSlider-thumb`,
not `.css-ae2u5c-MuiSlider-thumb`. Theme authors do it anyway, because nothing else reaches the
element [M]: **nine** of the 23 cloned repositories select generated `css-<hash>` classes:
`zombie-release` 111 distinct ones, `ZestyTheme` 110, `better-jellyfin-ui` 93, `Jellyfish` 42. GlassFin's work toward 12.0
([#35](https://github.com/KBH-Reeper/GlassFin/issues/35)) begins
`main.MuiBox-root.css-1iogify { margin-top: -100px !important; }`. A theme written that way breaks
whenever the style that produced the hash changes.

### 3.3 Elsewhere: the same curve

- **Kodi** [M]. Each release bumps the `xbmc.gui` version skins import (5.12 in Krypton to 5.18
  in Piers), and the official repository offers a skin only under the release it was built for.
  Skins listed per release: Krypton 29, Leia 21, Matrix 19, Nexus 22, Omega 13, Piers 14. Of 49
  distinct skins across the six, **three** are in all six. (Popular skins also ship from their
  authors' own repositories, so this undercounts what exists; it does not undercount the churn.)
- **Obsidian** [doc][M]. Its 1.0 release (2022-10-13,
  [changelog](https://obsidian.md/changelog/2022-10-13-desktop-v1.0.0/)): *"If you rely on any
  third party plugins or themes, we highly encourage you to check for updates before delving into
  Obsidian 1.0"*, and *"Themes that are not compatible with 1.0 have been marked as 'legacy' and
  cannot be installed until they get updated."* On 2022-10-01 the directory listed **32 of 131**
  themes as legacy; **18** still are four years later. Obsidian responded by narrowing the seam:
  its theme guide says *"Use CSS variables"* and exposes *"more than 400"* of them; its review
  checklist says *"Do use CSS variables whenever you can"* and *"Don't use `!important`"*
  (`obsidian-developer-docs`); and 1.0 added a built-in accent-colour picker, *"Theme support may
  vary."*
- **Discord** [M]. Client themes need a modified client (BetterDiscord, Vencord). `Slate` (228
  stars, archived): *"Slate is discontinued. This theme was too large to keep up to date with all
  of Discord massive changes."* `Comfy` (662 stars) and `ClearVision v6` (423) are archived.
- **Firefox** [doc]. *"Complete themes"* were retired with the other legacy add-ons for Firefox 57
  ([Mozilla Add-ons blog, 2017](https://blog.mozilla.org/addons/2017/02/16/the-road-to-firefox-57-compatibility-milestones/)),
  leaving themes that set colours and images only. Since Firefox 69, *"Firefox no longer loads
  userChrome.css or userContent.css by default"*; users who want them set a pref named
  `toolkit.legacyUserProfileCustomizations.stylesheets`
  ([release notes](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/69.0/releasenotes/)).
- **Discourse** [doc] built version pinning for its themes because they break: *"Theme and plugin
  developers generally want to target the latest release of Discourse, without worrying about
  backwards compatibility. But sites running older Discourse releases still need a version …
  that works"*, so Discourse checks out a `d-compat/<YYYY>.<M>` branch of the theme for each
  release ([Meta 272665](https://meta.discourse.org/t/272665)).
- **The counter-example is VS Code** [M]. Its themes are colour values for declared keys. *Atom One
  Dark Theme* was last updated in October 2022 and has 7.4 M installs; GitHub Theme was last
  updated in October 2024 and has 20.3 M. A theme written against a narrow typed seam keeps
  working without its author.

*Reading it (judgement):* the owner's experience is the norm, not bad luck. Wherever a theme can
reach the DOM or the widget tree, a major release leaves most themes broken for weeks to years,
and the vendors that took it seriously (Mozilla, Obsidian, Discourse, Kodi) either narrowed the
seam to values or built versioning machinery to cope. Where the seam is only values (VS Code,
Firefox's current themes) themes age well, but they can only change colour.

## 4. What themes are used for, and what does each job better

The jobs below are drawn from the READMEs coded in ui-design-practice §4.2 and the requests in §2.

| Job | Who needs it | Better served by | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Light or dark** | everyone, by context and eyesight | following the system setting; an in-app override only as *System / Light / Dark* | Apple HIG [doc] ([Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)): *"Avoid offering an app-specific appearance setting … they may think your app is broken because it doesn't respond to their systemwide appearance choice"*, but also *"it can make sense for an app that supports immersive media viewing to use a permanently dark appearance."* Android [doc] ([Dark theme](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/theming/darktheme)): *"Light, Dark, System default (the recommended default option)."* Only 2 of 18 Jellyfin themes offer a light variant. |
| **Higher contrast, low vision** | people with low vision | `prefers-contrast: more` (Baseline May 2022) and `forced-colors` (Baseline September 2022) [doc] ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-contrast), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors)); a true-black option, as Plex's High Contrast | Microsoft [doc] ([Contrast themes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/accessibility/high-contrast-themes)): contrast themes use *"a constrained color palette (with contrast ratios typically at or above 7:1)"*; *"Do not confuse contrast themes with light and dark themes."* In forced-colors mode `box-shadow` is *"forced to 'none'"* (MDN), so a focus ring drawn with a shadow disappears. A third-party theme cannot be trusted to keep contrast: the maintainers' complaint in §3.1. |
| **Colour vision** | about one man in twelve | the app's own palette, checked once; a problem colour never distinguished by hue alone (WCAG 1.4.1) | GitHub ships *"light and dark colorblind themes"* and a high-contrast theme as built-ins [doc] ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/setting-up-and-managing-your-personal-account-on-github/managing-user-account-settings/managing-your-theme-settings)). D251 §2 already keeps the problem colour a hue apart from the accent. |
| **Text size, spacing** | low vision, reading | the browser: zoom and font size, with layouts in `rem` that reflow (WCAG 1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12) | WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.8 (AAA) wants *"Foreground and background colors can be selected by the user"*, and its Understanding document adds *"The mechanism can be provided by the browser or other user agent. Content is not required to provide the mechanism"* [doc] ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)). |
| **Reduced motion** | vestibular disorders | `prefers-reduced-motion`, already honoured in `tokens.css` | WCAG 2.3.3, cited in ui-design-practice. |
| **Density, poster size** | taste, screen size | a setting (m5.md's open question on poster size), stored as a per-person preference | Plex's poster slider; 12 of 18 Jellyfin themes advertise spacing or alignment changes, which is a default the app should get right. |
| **Hiding features, rearranging the home** | taste, households with children | settings: row order is already user state (D138 §2) | `Jellyfin-Media-Bar` is a feature written in CSS (D138 §4); `better-jellyfin-ui` ships *"remove-logo"* and *"floating-header"* plugins [M]. |
| **Personal taste, branding** | enthusiasts; an admin who wants the server to feel theirs | a choice among a few accents or palettes maintained with the app | Ten of 18 Jellyfin themes advertise a choosable accent; Plex, Obsidian and Kodi's Estuary all ship this built in (§5). |

*Reading it (judgement):* every job except open-ended taste is served better by a built-in
setting or by the platform. The accessibility jobs in particular should not depend on a
third party: a theme that drops contrast or the focus ring is an accessibility defect the app's
tests (the D189 focus test, the planned contrast checks) cannot see.

## 5. Middle paths, and who took them

| Path | Who | How it went |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Follow the system's light/dark** | Apple's own apps, GitHub, Obsidian, VS Code | The platforms' recommendation (§4). Costs one more palette to keep correct, which is token values only once the tokens are roles (ui-architecture R4). |
| **Built-in palettes, maintained with the app** | Plex (2021: dark, light, high contrast, two accents), Jellyfin (six built-ins on one CSS-variable base in 12.0), Kodi Estuary (13 palettes), GitHub (light, dark, dimmed, high contrast, colour-blind) | Nobody reports these breaking; they are tested with the app. Plex's are behind tokens and drew little demand for more. |
| **Accent colour only** | Obsidian 1.0 (a colour picker), Plex (two accents, paid), half the Jellyfin themes | Cheap where the accent is one role token. The risk is contrast: a user-picked accent can fail 3:1 against the surface; a short list of pre-checked accents avoids it. |
| **High-contrast mode** | Plex (true black), GitHub, Windows forced colours | The accessibility job in §4; `prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors` let the app follow the system with no setting at all. |
| **A small declared set of variables, the rest unsupported** | Home Assistant, Obsidian | Home Assistant documents `primary-color`, `accent-color` and per-state colours as supported and says of every other variable: *"Although we do our best to keep things working, the behavior of other theme variables can change between releases"* [doc] ([Frontend](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/frontend/)). This is D138 §4's design, already run by someone else; its themes still break on the unsupported variables people use. |
| **User CSS as an unsupported escape hatch** | Jellyfin (server-wide field), Firefox (`userChrome.css`, behind a pref) | Jellyfin's is the source of §3.1. Firefox turned its off by default. |
| **No user CSS, a widened declared seam instead** | VS Code | Its maintainer, closing user stylesheets in 2018 [user] ([PR 40764](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/pull/40764#issuecomment-355011312)): *"this makes all of our CSS class names API (in the same way all of our themable colors have become API). we do not want to commit ourselves to keeping the CSS class names stable … the other issue is that bug reports we receive will be harder to understand if users run with custom CSS changes that we are not aware of."* Requests for a specific change become requests for a setting. |

## 6. What a public seam would cost Orpheum

- **Class names become API.** The standard already says so (*"a theme can find the rule"*), and
  VS Code's maintainer names the price: every rename is a breaking change to someone's theme. It
  conflicts with D245 §5 (*"the look is changed often, and a change to one part must not need
  fiddling with another"*) at exactly the time M5 iterates the look daily on staging.
- **It rules out the scoping tools.** CSS Modules and hashed names are excluded only for themes'
  sake (typescript.md). `@scope` and a prefix lint (ui-architecture R5, R10) would remain options
  either way, but the class names they scope would have to stay stable.
- **Tokens become API too.** D138 §4 wanted tokens *"versioned"*. Then renaming a role token
  (R4's split into scale and role tokens has not happened yet) is a breaking change needing the
  same discipline [D85](../../decisions/working-notes.md) gives the HTTP API: a diff in the gate,
  a deprecation window, a changelog. Home Assistant's split (§5) shows the half-measure: a few
  supported tokens and a large unsupported remainder people use anyway.
- **The tests multiply.** typescript.md and testing-strategy.md already run the D189 focus test
  *"in every shipped theme"*. A third-party theme is never shipped, so it is never tested, and
  a bug report from a themed client is a different program (VS Code's second reason).
- **Hashing is not a defence.** §3.2: if the app offers no seam and some users want one, they
  select hashed names and break on every build. That is their cost, not the app's, as long as
  the app has said it supports none (Jellyfin's *"Support is not provided"*).
- **What D138 §4 got right** stays true: an undeclared surface is still a surface people will
  use, and whatever Orpheum declares should be values, not selectors or script (D12's reasoning).

## 7. Recommendation for Orpheum

*Judgement throughout.* Two people use Orpheum first, and one skin is wanted (D245 §2). The
evidence says most people never change the look, that those who do are mostly asking for a
better default or a setting, and that third-party themes in this field break with every major
release (four of Jellyfin's top fifteen survived 12.0's first month). So:

1. **No user-facing theming seam: no third-party themes, no custom-CSS field, no promise of
   stable class names or tokens.** The tokens remain the skin's single source (ui-architecture
   R4; D251), changed freely while M5 iterates. Reason: §3 (themes rot), §6 (a seam freezes the
   names M5 needs to change), and §2 (demand is small and mostly for a better default).
2. **Do the jobs themes do as settings and platform features, built in and tested:**
   - honour `prefers-reduced-motion` (done), `prefers-contrast: more` (a raised-contrast set of
     token values), and `forced-colors` (check the client is usable in Windows contrast themes;
     the focus ring's outer colour is a `box-shadow`, which forced colours remove, and its
     `outline` must carry it alone);
   - when a light palette comes (D251 §1, later), it **follows the system** with a
     *System / Dark / Light* choice per person, defaulting to System, as Android advises. Until
     then dark only, which Apple's guidance allows for immersive media;
   - density and poster size as per-person settings if m5.md's poster-size question says yes;
   - layouts in `rem` so browser zoom and font size work.
3. **Later, and only if wanted: a few built-in accents** (say three to five pre-checked against
   the surfaces for 3:1 and 4.5:1), as Plex, Obsidian and Kodi do. One role token makes it cheap;
   it is taste, so not in Cut A.
4. **Revisit at public release** (D195 names the product provisional; D220 makes public release a
   separate step). If people then ask for themes, the next step is §5's Home Assistant path, a
   short, versioned list of supported role tokens, not CSS.

## 8. Where the evidence is weak

- **No usage data.** No media server publishes how many installations use a custom theme.
  Stars and votes measure enthusiasts and requesters, not users; Jellyfin's custom CSS is set
  server-wide, so one admin's choice covers a household.
- **The Jellyfin 12 table is one month old** and judged from commit messages, READMEs and issues,
  not by installing each theme on 12.x. "Updated" means the authors say they support the new
  layout; open issues show even those are partial. A month is also short: most of the
  "no update" group may yet update, or may be abandoned.
- **Obsidian's 2022-10-01 legacy count** is from a snapshot two weeks before 1.0 shipped; the
  flag's meaning is taken from the 1.0 changelog. Its history for the period after was not
  extracted (the blob-less history walk was stopped for time).
- **Kodi's per-release skin counts** cover the official repository only.
- **Plex's forum** was searched by title only; Reddit, where much theme talk happens, was not
  reachable in earlier research and was not tried again. Emby's forum returned 403.
- **The issue-keyword count** (26 of 108) is a crude title match.
- **"About one man in twelve"** for colour-vision deficiency is the commonly cited figure, not
  re-sourced here.
