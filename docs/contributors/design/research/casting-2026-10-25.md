## 1. The answer in short

- **Yes for a first step, but only the first family is free.** Extending a laptop's desktop to
  the TV (or mirroring it) works today with no code: the laptop is the player, so Watch Together,
  voice, sync, subtitles and our quality ladder all work as they do now. What it does not give is
  a phone as the remote, or a laptop screen that stays free: the player page, with its controls,
  is on the TV (§2.1, §5.1).
- **Netflix's black screen is DRM, which we do not use.** Netflix and the DRM'd services demand
  HDCP on the output and refuse mirroring; nothing in Orpheum asks for it, so mirroring and
  extending just work (§2.4).
- **Casting proper (the TV fetches the film; the sender stays a remote) is what the split-device
  sketch wants, and it is not free.** AirPlay video needs a URL the TV can fetch with no cookie and
  no service worker, so a credential in the URL, against D154's rule, and it reaches the core only
  at home or where the core has a public direct path. A custom Cast receiver can be our own web
  player and join the group as a playback device, which is D124's design, but whether WebRTC
  works inside a Cast receiver (our only way to a core away from home, D314) is undocumented
  (§3).
- **Fire TV has neither Cast nor AirPlay**: Miracast mirroring only, and on the newest Vega sticks
  only from Android. Fire TV's own browser already plays (closed-beta.md B5), so for it the
  `orpheum.cc` code of the split-device sketch stays the route (§2.5, §4).
- **Recommended order (judgement)**: tell testers to extend today; then the custom Cast receiver,
  joined by the Cast session itself; AirPlay video for watching alone at home; the `orpheum.cc`
  code for every TV with a browser and no Cast. AirPlay is not "near-free" as hoped: on our player
  it needs a native-HLS switch, a URL-borne grant and subtitles in the HLS manifest (§5).

## 2. The three families

### 2.1 Extend the desktop

- **Windows**: Win+P offers *"Duplicate — See the same thing on all your displays. Extend — See
  your desktop across multiple screens … Second screen only"*, and "Connect to a wireless display"
  reaches any Miracast receiver (Microsoft support) [1P]. A Fire TV, many Android TVs and many
  smart TVs are Miracast receivers [J].
- **macOS**: Control Centre, Screen Mirroring, the TV, then *"choose whether to share a window or
  to mirror or extend your Mac screen on your TV"*; both devices *"connected to the same network"*
  (Apple, Mac User Guide, "Stream video and audio with AirPlay") [1P]. The receiver is an Apple TV
  or an AirPlay 2 TV (§2.5). **Sharing one window** is newer and fits our case well: the player
  window goes to the TV, the rest stays on the Mac [I].
- **What it gives us**: the TV is a second monitor. Our page plays in a browser window dragged to
  it, full screen there. The laptop decodes, so every Orpheum feature works unchanged: the hub,
  sync, voice, JASSUB's subtitles, our ABR, boost and night mode, loudness levelling [I].
- **Latency** is the encoder's and the link's: figures of 20–200 ms for Miracast and AirPlay
  mirroring are quoted by vendors' blogs, none measured to a method we could check [J]. Audio and
  picture are delayed together when the sound also goes to the TV. Against D66's 60–3000 ms sync
  budget this is small, so a mirrored viewer stays in sync with the group [I].
- **Quality**: the film is decoded, composited and re-encoded as a screen stream, at the link's
  bitrate and the desktop's frame rate: softer than the source, with judder where 24 fps meets a
  60 Hz desktop. UNVERIFIED in degree; the device lab would see it at once [I].
- **Battery and heat**: the sender decodes the film and encodes the screen for the whole film.
  Fine for a laptop on its charger, poor for a phone [I].
- **Controls and voice stay on the sender's hardware**, keyboard, mouse and microphone, but the
  page's controls are drawn on the TV, where the window is (§5.1).
- **Sound follows the operating system's output**, which switches to the wireless display. Our
  page cannot send voice to the laptop and the film to the TV: no code in `web/` calls
  `setSinkId` [I, from the tree]. Voice therefore comes from the TV too, and the laptop's
  microphone hears the room: D63's echo, softened by D281's Talk, which opens the microphone only
  while held.

### 2.2 Mirror the screen

- **Miracast**: Windows, many Android phones (often under "Cast" or "Wireless display"), to Fire
  TV and many TVs [J]. Not on iPhone or iPad: *"Miracast is not available on iPhone or iPad and
  does not play DRM protected content"* (Uscreen help, on Vega Fire TV) [1P for their product, J
  for Amazon's].
- **AirPlay mirroring**: iPhone, iPad and Mac, to an Apple TV or an AirPlay 2 TV [1P].
- **Chromecast "Cast tab" or "Cast screen"** from Chrome desktop: the tab is encoded and sent; it
  is the slowest of the three, 300–600 ms in one vendor's blog [J], and 0.3–3 s for a Chrome tab
  in a 2026 study by a game emulator's author (gbe issue #48) [U].
- **What it gives us**: the same as §2.1, with the sender's own screen tied up. From a phone it
  means the phone shows the film full screen for two hours, screen on, decoding and encoding:
  the opposite of the owner's "phone as voice and remote" [I].

### 2.3 Cast: the TV fetches the film

The sender hands the TV a URL and then only sends commands; the TV fetches and plays. D124 chose
this shape: *"The sender does not relay bytes"*, and *"the phone can sleep"*.

- **Google Cast.**
  - A **Web Receiver** is an HTML page the Cast device loads; a custom one is *"registered using a
    distinct app ID"* (Cast overview) [1P]. Registration costs a one-time, *"non-refundable"* $5
    (Cast registration FAQ) [1P]. Until published, a receiver loads only on devices registered by
    serial number (Cast registration) [1P], as D192 §2 planned for the internal stage.
  - **HLS in CAF is now Shaka Player.** Google's migration guide: *"starting with an opt-in API in
    Oct '22 and changing the default in Nov '25"*, `useShakaForHls` now true by default; MPL
    *"issues will not be fixed"* [1P]. fMP4 segments are declared by
    `HlsSegmentFormat.FMP4` (Cast streaming protocols) [1P]. That answers cast-protocol.md §3's
    "unresolved risk" (fMP4 failing on MPL receivers) for current firmware, UNVERIFIED on old
    dongles.
  - **A custom receiver may play with its own player**: it is a web page, and projects run
    hls.js in one (search summaries of Bitmovin's and others' notes) [S]. On which Cast hardware
    hls.js keeps up, and whether CAF's media status still works with it, was not found:
    UNVERIFIED.
  - **Senders from the web**: the Web Sender SDK runs in *"Cast-supported web browsers on Mac,
    Windows, Linux, ChromeOS, and Android"*; *"Casting is not supported on the iOS Chrome
    browser"* (Cast web sender guide) [1P]. Chromium browsers only [S]. So no iPhone and no Safari
    sends Cast from a web page, which D188 §3 path 2 already noted.
  - **The W3C APIs**: the Remote Playback API (`video.remote.prompt()`) shipped on desktop Chrome
    in M121 (blink-dev, 2023-11) [1P], but it remotes a `src` URL; an element fed by MSE, as ours
    is by hls.js, has a blob URL that means nothing to a TV [S, I]. The Presentation API launches
    a receiver page by URL; Chrome on Android used it for Cast (Chrome blog, "Google Cast for
    Chrome on Android") [1P]. Neither lets us skip the custom receiver for a group [I].
  - **The Default Media Receiver** needs no registration and plays HLS with WebVTT (D137), but it
    cannot join the hub, apply our loudness gain or run our ABR [I].
- **AirPlay video from Safari.**
  - The element's AirPlay button, `x-webkit-airplay="allow"` and
    `webkitShowPlaybackTargetPicker()` hand the TV the video's URL (Apple's AirPlay guide) [1P].
  - **Not with MSE.** *"AirPlay cannot carry bytes fed from JavaScript"*; with hls.js, Safari
    offers AirPlay only if the element also carries a `<source>` with the HLS URL, which the TV
    then plays (hls.js #6482, #6197, the maintainer's workaround) [U, from a maintainer]. Our
    player uses hls.js wherever MSE exists, Safari on the Mac included (`hlsMethod` in
    `web/packages/player/src/hls.ts`), so AirPlay needs a switch to the native URL when a
    wireless target is chosen.
  - **No cookies reach the TV**: *"AirPlay does not support authentication cookies. AirPlay
    streams run on a device other than the device initiating the session"* (THEOplayer's AirPlay
    limitations, Dolby OptiView docs) [1P for their player]. Apple's guide warns the viewer *"must
    authenticate on the device that is relaying the video"* and to plan *"how long cookies or other
    authentication tokens should last"* [1P]. A 2026 open-source project (friendflix #112) solved
    it as everyone does: *"swaps to the token URL (the TV has no cookies)"* [U].
  - **Subtitles must be in the HLS manifest**: AirPlay *"does not support … sideloaded (WebVTT)
    subtitles"* (Dolby OptiView) [1P]. Our master playlist carries audio renditions only
    (`internal/core/chunks/playlists.go`), and subtitles are drawn by JASSUB on the page.
  - **No manual quality**: *"viewers can only rely on automatic quality selection"* [1P], so our
    ABR and its Watch Together pacing (D262 §2) do not apply; the TV's own AVPlayer chooses [I].
  - **Latency**: the TV buffers HLS as it likes; gbe's study puts AirPlay HLS at "2 s or more"
    [U]. Fine to watch alone; coarse for a group (§4.2).
- **DLNA/UPnP**: a TV's media renderer can be told a URL by a phone, LAN only, no authentication
  model worth the name, no subtitles to speak of, latency of "several seconds" (gbe #48) [U, I].
  Orpheum's server is not reached that way and nothing here argues for it. Noted, not proposed.

### 2.4 DRM and HDCP: why Netflix goes black and we would not

- Netflix's help page: *"Airplay and Screen Mirroring are no longer supported by Netflix"*, and for
  cables *"Make sure your video cable or adapter supports HDCP 2.2"* [1P]. DRM'd players require
  an HDCP-protected output; a mirroring or capture path is not one, so the video layer goes black
  while sound may continue [J].
- **Orpheum uses no DRM and no EME**, so nothing asks the output for HDCP, and mirroring,
  extending and capture show our film [I, from the tree: no EME in `web/packages/player`].
  Vega Fire TV's Miracast *"does not play DRM protected content"* (Uscreen) [1P], which does not
  touch us either.

## 3. What casting would need from Orpheum

### 3.1 Reaching the core

- **At home**, a cast TV is on the core's LAN, and our core is reached at its LAN name, an HTTPS
  name in public DNS pointing at a private address (discovery-protocol.md §3.3). Many routers drop
  such answers (rebinding protection), which the protocol already notes for every device [1P, ours].
  A Chromecast is reported to trust neither self-signed certificates nor local DNS names (gbe #48,
  citing dashkiosk) [U]; our name is neither, but whether a Chromecast resolves it through a router
  that filters it is UNVERIFIED.
- **Away from home** (a guest's TV) there is no public HTTPS name to fetch: D314 §3 says *"A core
  is reached through its discovery over WebRTC"*, with no tunnel. Our away client runs over
  WebRTC data channels, and a service worker on the page carries what the browser itself loads
  (webrtc-transport.md W2b).
  - **A custom Cast receiver could do the same**, being a Chrome page, if Chrome on the Cast device
    exposes `RTCPeerConnection` to a receiver. Google documents WebRTC on *"the Google Nest Smart
    Display and Chromecast with Google TV devices"* for its own camera streams (Google Home
    developers, CameraStream) [1P]; for a custom Web Receiver, *"no official documentation and no
    public project confirms it"* (gbe #48, October 2026) [U]. **UNVERIFIED, and it decides
    whether Cast works away from home.**
  - **Service workers in a Cast receiver**: not found in Google's documentation. The receiver
    need not depend on one: its hls.js could load through the SDK's transport directly, as the page
    does for its API calls, rather than through the worker [I]. UNVERIFIED either way.
  - **AirPlay away from home** works only when the core has a direct path the TV can reach (its
    published IPv6 or a mapped public IPv4, discovery-protocol.md §3.3's table). Behind carrier NAT
    with no IPv6 it cannot, since the TV fetches a URL and runs none of our code [I].
- **The sender cannot carry the bytes instead**: D124 declined the phone as a relay, and Cast and
  AirPlay give a sender nothing to serve from [1P, D124].

### 3.2 The credential

- **Cast**: D154 §2 already names *"`LoadRequestData.credentials` plus a request filter"* for our
  receiver, and §3 a `cast` grant: *"one cast session's queue … the sender, or session end"*. Our
  receiver is our code, so it sends the bearer header our transport already uses; no URL carries a
  secret [1P, ours].
- **AirPlay**: the TV is not our code. It fetches the master playlist, the media playlists and
  every chunk by URL, with no cookie and no header we set, so **the credential has to be in the
  URL**: the playlist path carries a short-lived, one-film, one-rendition grant, and every URL in
  the playlists the core writes carries it too [I].
  - That is D154 §2's *"`link` grant, minted on purpose — one item, one rendition, minutes"*, the
    case D154 kept for exporting to VLC; but D154 §6 also says *"no endpoint reads a token from a
    query string or a path segment, and a test asserts it"*. The two need reconciling by the owner
    (Q2).
  - **D217 §2's media token is not that grant**: it *"grants nothing"*, and *"every byte request
    still carries the media cookie or the Authorization header"*. It is the reference the grant
    would name.
  - D135's warning applies with force: a URL on the TV can be read by anything on the LAN, and
    the grant should reach only what is playing [1P, ours].

### 3.3 Playing our fMP4 ladder

- **Our own receiver** (judgement): the receiver is `@orpheum/player` in another shell, as the
  README and D124 say, so it keeps our ABR, Watch Together's following and D226's loudness gain.
  CAF's Shaka would play the same HLS (§2.3) but takes the choice of rendition and the control of
  loading away from us, and our group following needs hls.js (`canFollowAGroup`,
  `web/packages/player/src/hls.ts`) [I].
  - **Performance of hls.js on Cast hardware** was not found: UNVERIFIED. A Chromecast with Google
    TV and a Google TV Streamer are Android TV devices whose Chrome runs MSE, so it is likely fine
    there and doubtful on an old dongle [I].
- **AirPlay**: AVPlayer plays HLS with fMP4 natively, and our separate audio renditions are
  `EXT-X-MEDIA` groups it understands; it chooses the rendition itself [1P for AVPlayer, I].

### 3.4 Subtitles and sound

- **Cast**: D137 decided the receiver *"ships no subtitle renderer"*: WebVTT with Cast's
  `TextTrackStyle`, and ASS or PGS by D78's server-rendered overlay. So JASSUB on a Chromecast is
  not the plan, and its cost there was not looked at.
- **AirPlay**: subtitles only as `EXT-X-MEDIA TYPE=SUBTITLES` with segmented WebVTT in the master
  playlist, which the core does not write today; ASS styling is lost unless burned in [I].
- **Boost and night mode** are server mixes (D217 §3 prepares a standard and a boosted rendition
  of each audio track), so they reach any receiver as an audio rendition. **Loudness levelling**
  is a gain the player applies (D226), so our Cast receiver keeps it; the Default Media Receiver
  and AirPlay lose it [I].

## 4. A cast TV in Watch Together, and alone

### 4.1 Cast: the session is the pairing

- **The receiver is a playback device of the sender's member**, exactly the split-device
  sketch's §4.2 grant (a credential with a fixed `playback` role, ending with the session), and
  D124's table row "Cast".
- **The Cast session replaces the code.** The person is in the session on their laptop (or an
  Android phone in Chrome); they press Cast and pick the TV. The sender then sends the receiver,
  over a custom Cast message namespace, the session's locator and a playback-only credential the
  core mints for it. The receiver connects to the core itself (at home, its LAN name; away,
  WebRTC through the discovery, §3.1) and attaches to the hub as `playback` [I].
  - It needs no `orpheum.cc`, no typed code and no check code: the person chose the TV in the
    system's own picker, on a LAN they share with it.
  - The LAN is not a trust boundary: D135 says *"nothing authenticates the sender"* on Cast, so
    any device on the network can talk to the receiver. The credential is still playback-only,
    one session, and ends with it (sketch §4.9) [I].
- **Sync**: the receiver runs our player and follows the group's clock as any follower does
  (D250); the hop through Cast's status, which D124 feared would eat the sync budget, is not on
  the path, because the receiver speaks to the hub directly and the sender only relays the
  person's commands through the hub (D124: *"the receiver holds the `playback` membership and
  speaks the six methods directly"*). D88's group of one is the same with no group [I].
- **The sender becomes the remote**: its player detaches and its page shows voice and the
  controls, which is the sketch's §4.3 page. The same page serves the `orpheum.cc` route, so
  building it once serves both [I].
- **Who can cast**: Chrome or Edge on a laptop, Chrome on Android. Not an iPhone (§2.3). For the
  owner's case (D188 §3, her MacBook and iPhone) that is the laptop starting the cast once and
  her iPhone joining for voice, which needs the sketch's voice-on-another-device work too.

### 4.2 AirPlay: the sender stays the group's device

- The Apple TV or AirPlay TV runs none of our code, so it cannot join the hub. The Safari page
  stays the member's playback device, and its `<video>` is a proxy for the TV: Safari reports the
  remote position and passes play, pause and seek [1P, I].
- **For a group it is coarse**: our group following works only through hls.js (`canFollowAGroup`
  in `web/packages/player/src/hls.ts`, citing D250 §7: *"Below that an iPhone has only its own
  HLS, which watches alone"*), and on AirPlay the element plays
  natively. The page could only seek the TV when it drifts, behind a buffer of seconds (§2.3).
  UNVERIFIED how far it drifts, and whether a seek every so often is watchable [I].
- **For watching alone it is fine**: the Mac or iPhone is the remote, the TV fetches, and the
  sender can lock or sleep once Safari hands over, which is AirPlay's normal behaviour for video
  URLs [I, UNVERIFIED for a locked iPhone with Safari in the background].
- Voice stays on the sender, and the film comes from the TV's speakers, so the earbuds question
  of the sketch's §4.10 applies [I].

### 4.3 Extend or mirror: nothing to join

The laptop is the device; the TV is its monitor. Nothing in the group changes, and nothing needs
building. Its limits are the sender's: the page on the TV, the sound on one output (§2.1).

### 4.4 TVs with neither (Fire TV above all)

Fire TV's own browser plays our client (closed-beta.md B5, the owner), and it has neither Cast
nor AirPlay, so it joins by the sketch's `orpheum.cc` code. Miracast from a Windows laptop is the
no-code fallback [1P, J].

## 5. Recommendation

### 5.1 What testers can do today (no code)

**Judgement:** this is a fair first step for the closed beta, for Watch Together and alone, as
long as testers know its limits.

1. **Windows laptop**: Win+K (or Win+P, "Connect to a wireless display"), pick the TV; Win+P,
   **Extend**. **Mac**: Control Centre, Screen Mirroring, the Apple TV or AirPlay TV, **extend**,
   or share only the browser window. **Chromecast**: Chrome's Cast menu, Cast tab, as a last
   resort (the slowest and softest, §2.2).
2. Open Orpheum in the browser, drag the window to the TV, start the film, and press the
   player's full-screen button there.
3. **Sound**: let it all go to the TV. Use **Talk** (D281) rather than an open microphone, so the
   laptop does not send the film back; earbuds cannot be used for voice alone, because the
   laptop's sound goes to one output (§2.1).
4. **Controls**: the keyboard and mouse still work, but the controls are drawn on the TV. Move the
   pointer across to the TV's screen, or use the player's keys with the window focused. The
   laptop's own screen shows nothing of the film.
5. **Caveats**: keep the laptop on its charger; prefer 5 GHz Wi-Fi; expect a softer picture than
   playing on the TV itself; a phone used this way must keep its screen on for the whole film;
   iPhones can mirror only to AirPlay receivers, and Fire TV takes only Miracast.

### 5.2 Build order

The device tests that come before choosing (the owner, 2026-10-25: test first) are planned in
[tv-device-tests.md](../plans/tv-device-tests.md), with the lab pages they use.

All efforts are **rough, unverified estimates** in focused days of the owner with agents, by
analogy with this tree's own history; none was planned in detail.

1. **The remote page** (the sketch's §4.3: voice, the timeline, the person's subtitles and
   volume, with no player) and the core's playback-only credential (sketch §4.2). Every route
   below needs both, so they come first. About 1–2 weeks.
2. **The custom Cast receiver** in `web/apps/cast-receiver`: CAF's context for the session and
   the custom namespace, our player inside, attach as `playback`, WebVTT with `TextTrackStyle`
   (D137). **First a one-day spike on a Chromecast with Google TV**: does `RTCPeerConnection`
   work, does hls.js play our ladder smoothly, does the LAN name resolve. About 1.5–3 weeks after
   the spike if WebRTC works; at home only if it does not.
3. **AirPlay from Safari, for watching alone at home**: the native-URL switch on a wireless
   target, a URL-borne one-film grant (after Q2), subtitle renditions in the master playlist.
   About 1 week, more if subtitles need the playlist writer reworked. Not the near-free step it
   looks like (§2.3).
4. **The `orpheum.cc` code** of the split-device sketch, for Fire TV and every TV whose browser
   plays and which has no Cast: it reuses steps 1's page and credential. The sketch's §4.11 lists
   the rest.

Why this order (judgement): extending covers the closed beta's living room today at no cost; the
remote page is shared by every later route; Cast is D124's chosen first receiver, and its
pairing is the system picker, which is simpler for a person than a code; AirPlay cannot join a
group well, so it comes after the route that can. If the spike finds no WebRTC in a receiver,
the `orpheum.cc` code moves ahead of Cast for the away case, since a guest's TV is D124's
motivating one.

## 6. Open questions for the owner

1. **Is extend-to-TV the closed beta's answer for the living room until B5**, with §5.1's recipe
   written into the testers' help?
2. **A credential in a URL for AirPlay**: D154 §2 allows a purpose-minted `link` grant (one item,
   one rendition, minutes); §6 forbids any endpoint reading a token from a path or query. Does
   AirPlay get such a grant, and is §6 amended for it? Without it, AirPlay cannot play.
3. **AirPlay's scope**: watching alone at home only, or also as a coarse member of a group,
   seeking the TV when it drifts?
4. **The Cast spike first**: one day on a Chromecast with Google TV to settle WebRTC and hls.js
   in a receiver, before any receiver is built?
5. **Cast away from home without WebRTC**: if the spike fails, is a guest's Chromecast left to the
   `orpheum.cc` route (its browser, if it has one; a Chromecast has none by default, D188 §3), or
   does it wait for a relay or a public name (D314 §3)?
6. **Is the Cast session enough pairing**, with no code or TV-side confirmation, given D135's
   unauthenticated sender channel, or does the receiver also show "‹person› is casting" with a
   Leave?
7. **Sound on a laptop that extends**: worth routing voice to a chosen output (`setSinkId`), so
   earbuds carry voice while the TV carries the film? It is a small client change, but it adds a
   choice to the speaker question (D184).
8. **Fire TV**: since it takes only Miracast and its browser, is it the `orpheum.cc` route's first
   target, ahead of Samsung and LG?
