# M4: two people, one remote

- **Status:** IN-PROGRESS — phases planned 2026-10-05 from [D243](../../decisions/working-notes.md)
  and cut-a's M4 row; A–H built, the acceptance waiting on the owner's answers in
  [M4's audit](../research/audit-2026-10-06.md) §8.
- **Depends on:** [cut-a.md](cut-a.md), [watch-together.md](watch-together.md),
  [remote-access.md](remote-access.md), [auth.md](auth.md)

cut-a's M4 row asks for the HTTPS name, reachability, invites, Watch Together over §5.1, voice
through embedded TURN, recording for signed-in members, now playing, and scans and backups as jobs
with scheduled backups. D243 answers the nine questions D223 §11 left. This plan orders the work
so that each phase ends with `make check-pr` green and the dev loop working, and so that what a
later phase stands on, above all who may see what, is built first.

Every phase: behaviour changes carry tests that fail when reverted; schema changes follow D228's
fold rule; a decision's Status line changes only once its clauses are read back against the code.
D243 §5, rebroadcasting, is designed and not built: what it asks of the data is held now
(`TestArch_IDsAreNeverGivenTwice`, and §A's per-library tick).

## Phase A — who may see what

D243 §4; D130, D174 §1, D218, D154.

- **A role's libraries.** A table of the libraries each role may browse and play, each with its
  *may rebroadcast* tick (stored now, offered when §5 is built). The built-in admin role sees
  every library and cannot be narrowed; every other role sees only its list. The admin makes,
  renames and deletes roles (a role a person holds cannot be deleted), sets each one's libraries
  and capabilities, and gives each person one role. cut-a §1's "no roles beyond admin and user"
  is revised by D243 §4: the two built-in roles stay, and the admin may add more. The role's
  `predicate` column stays for the deferred rule compiler (D164, D193); a library list is the
  first, structural restriction.
- **One check.** Every list, search, title, collection, play, byte and artwork request asks one
  function of the caller which libraries it may reach, at the time of the request, and answers as
  if the others did not exist: `not_found`, never `forbidden`. A playback started before access
  was taken back stops at its next byte request. A new library is reached by the admin role
  alone until a role is given it.
- **Invites.** The admin sets an account up in full (a name, a password, a role), or issues an
  invitation under a role with a note only the admin sees of who it is for; its person fills in
  their own name and password, and the note stays with the account (D246 §1–§2). There is no
  outsider kind of account. The link's secret goes after `#` (D243 §3), and it works once.
- **Acceptance:** an invite works once; taking a library back from a role ends its members'
  access to it at their next request; a library a person may not reach is absent from every
  answer, search and Continue Watching included.

## Phase B — scheduled backups, and scans and backups in the job view

D243 §11; D223 §6; cut-a's M4 row.

- A `backup` job kind, one at a time: a scheduled backup every `backup_every_days` at
  `backup_hour_local_tz` in the server's `time_zone` (D246 §7), never holding `secret.key`, and the last `backups_kept` kept; pre-restore and
  pre-migration copies apart and never pruned. *Back up now* on the System page starts a job, its
  key checkbox kept, and the job view shows it. A scan's jobs show in the job view (they are jobs
  since D240; this checks the view names them).

## Phase C — now playing

cut-a §5's Running it row; D243 §9's last sentence.

- `GET /now-playing` lists each playback in progress: who, which device, what, how (direct or
  prepared, and why), and since when. `POST /now-playing/{id}/stop` with a message: the player
  hears it in the answer to its next play report, within one report interval, stops and shows the
  message.

## Phase D — the HTTPS name

D243 §6; remote-access.md.

- Config keys for the domain, the ACME contact, and the HTTPS listen address. The Cloudflare API
  token is a sealed secret (D194). ACME DNS-01 with `golang.org/x/crypto/acme`, the TXT record set
  and removed through two Cloudflare API calls on the standard library and `outbound`; the
  certificate and its key kept in the instance's persistent directory, renewed by a job well
  before expiry, and served by the HTTPS listener. The plain listener stays for the house and the
  dev loop.
- Tested against a fake ACME server and a fake Cloudflare in the test, never the network.

**Built** (2026-10-05): `internal/core/providers/cloudflare` (the two calls, with go.md §21's
failure policy), `internal/core/certificate` (ACME DNS-01, the certificate kept in the persistent
directory, a `renew_certificate` job when there is none or it expires within a month, asked for
by a daily sweep and at start), the `[https]` config table, and an HTTPS listener beside the plain
one serving the current certificate. Fakes: `certificatetest` (a CA that checks the challenge's
record) and `cloudflaretest`. The System page says when the certificate expires, or that there
is none yet. Not yet: the owner's first real certificate, which needs the VPS and the token
(Phase G).

## Phase E — Watch Together

D243 §1–§3, §9; watch-together.md; cut-a §5.1; D117–D125, D188, D191.

- **E1, the session.** Create (the host's item, abilities, cap), the join link (its secret after
  `#`), memberships, rotate, eject, the cap counting people; a membership's devices and roles.
- **E2, the wire.** `GET /together/ws` on `github.com/coder/websocket`: §5.1's messages, the
  server's sequence, group state (`Idle`, `Waiting`, `Paused`, `Playing`), conflicts and
  `rejected`, suspensions, ping and the minimum-RTT sample.
- **E3, the guest's bytes.** An anonymous guest's media cookie covers the session's one film for
  the membership's life, moves when the film changes, and is revoked by ejection or the end
  (D243 §1); a guest reaches no library.
- **E4, recording.** A membership is a person's when any of its devices joined signed in, sending
  its credential in its first `hello` (D243 §2); the playing device's `state` reports are that
  person's spans. An anonymous membership records nothing.
- **E5, the client.** The SDK's sync engine over the six methods (watch-together.md), the web
  player as the first client, the lobby with one **Join**, named events, the roster, a drop as a
  state, two volume controls.

**Built** (2026-10-05), E1 and E2: `internal/core/together` (sessions in memory, memberships and
their credentials kept as hashes, the group state machine, stale seeks refused, suspensions, a
ping's pong, each device's reported round trip in the roster, and voice signalling relayed), the
session endpoints and `GET /together/ws` in `internal/core/api`. §5.1's wire form is camelCase,
as the rest of the API.

**Rebuilt** (2026-10-05) for D246, which replaced D121's abilities, the shared link with its cap
and rotation, and D243 §1's guest cookie: the host opens a session with nothing on (or has their
one session back), makes one link per person with a note of who it is for, which only the host
sees, and alone controls playback, putting a film on, pausing, seeking and stopping. A viewer
joins by their link with a name of their own, and their membership credential is what their
player plays by: `POST /together/playback` starts a playback of the film on, in the host's
libraries, and sets a viewer cookie beside the media cookie for the byte paths; a playback of the
film before stops once another is on. A device joining or loading holds no one up until it has
once been ready. E3 is this; E4 (recording a signed-in member's viewing) waits with D246 §4's
signed-in recipients. Not yet: E5, the client.

**Built** (2026-10-05), E5, the web client: the SDK's Watch Together client
(`packages/sdk/src/together*.ts`: the connection says hello, pings and keeps the minimum round
trip, reports its player's state on every change and every minute, reconnects with a growing wait;
a player follows its group, nudging its rate for drift of 60 ms to 3 s and seeking beyond, and
holds no one up until it has once been where the group is); the viewer's page at `/together`, for
someone not signed in (their link's secret after `#`, their name, the speaker question at once,
kept in the browser, then only the playback, black while nothing plays, their volume and
**Leave**, with named events and who is here); the host's **Watch Together** screen
(`/watch-together`: start, a link per person with a note and its QR code, withdraw, remove, end);
and the play page leading the host's session while the tab hosts one (the film goes on for
everyone from where it starts, the host's play, pause and seeks are commands, the host's video
waits while a viewer buffers, leaving the player stops it). The menu shows Watch Together to
everyone signed in, since `/me` does not say whether a role may host; the server refuses the
others. Not yet: voice (Phase F's client), text chat, subtitles and levelling for a viewer, undoing
a seek, the drift thresholds measured in the device lab.

**Next for viewers** (the owner, 2026-10-05): subtitles. *"For the first testing version of view
together it's fine to not have subtitles, but I'll need them soon after once we have the kinks
out."* The viewer's player shows none yet, nor levelling or an audio-track menu.

## Phase F — voice

D243 §8; watch-together.md's Voice; D193 §4.

- Embedded TURN with `github.com/pion/turn`: port 3478, relays 49160–49199, credentials per
  membership that end with it (`GET /together/{id}/ice`), the relay's public address a config key.
  Signalling on the session WebSocket (`rtc`). The web client's microphone, per-source gain and
  the film/partner volume pair.

**Built** (2026-10-05), the relay: `internal/core/turn` (pion/turn on `[turn] listen`, relaying
through `relay_port_min`–`relay_port_max` at `public_address`; credentials naming one membership
and an expiry, their password derived from the TURN secret, which the server makes when it has
none it can open; admitted only while the membership lasts), started by `serve` when
`public_address` is set. `GET /together/{id}/ice` hands each member, by their membership
credential, credentials of their own, and none when the server relays nothing.

**Built** (2026-10-06), the web client's voice, a prototype the owner tried between two phones:
**Talk** attaches a second device of the membership in the `voice` role and keeps one call with
each other person's voice device (`packages/sdk/src/together-voice*.ts`); Mute, Leave voice, and
the partner's volume, up to four times as loud as it came. Voice starts off, is turned on and off
at any time, and a microphone that will not open or a call that cannot connect turns it off with
the reason, the browser's own words beside a refused microphone; Talk tries again (the owner). The
browser's processing of the microphone is a setting, off since the owner's Android test, where it
turned the film down and took the volume buttons. VQ21 and VQ23.

## Phase G — reachability

D243 §7; remote-access.md's three questions.

- The VPS and WireGuard configuration for the owner (a document and the files, not code): the
  tunnel from the core's machine, the forwarding of TCP 443, TCP and UDP 3478 and the relay range,
  replies routed back through the tunnel. The System page's reachability check, from the outside
  in.

**Built** (2026-10-05): `deploy/vps` (the VPS's and the core machine's WireGuard ends, the VPS's
port forwards, the `[https]` and `[turn]` tables, and the steps), and the System page's
reachability check (`internal/core/reachability`, `POST /system/reachability`): the core asks its
own name for the echo of a nonce, so "reached" is the request coming back to this server, and a
STUN binding at the relay's port over UDP and TCP; a failure names its hop. The relay itself
now answers from the tunnel's address and relays only to the public internet. The
owner's VPS, and a relayed call across it, are VQ19.

## Phase H — M4's acceptance

cut-a's M4 row; D243 §9; D252, which settles how the profiles run and what is measured.

- Two Playwright clients stay within 200 ms of each other 95% of the time for 30 minutes under the
  three netem profiles; fake-microphone voice arrives one-way under 300 ms with under 2% audible
  loss; no credential appears in any URL of a HAR capture; a signed-in guest's history advances
  and an anonymous one's does not. The netem profiles run in the sandbox's containers for the
  gate; the owner's remote-household VM on nuc13 (device-lab.md) confirms them as a
  verification-queue item.
- The milestone audit (audit-checklist.md), then M5, the polished UI (D245).

**Built** (2026-10-06): in the browser tier (`web/e2e/together.spec.ts`, Chrome and Firefox), a
link that works once and only once joined, after a reload; no cookie or stored credential of host
or guest in any address of their HAR captures; and voice between two fake microphones within
D243 §9, measured from the browsers' own WebRTC statistics. `make test-acceptance`
(`web/e2e/acceptance.spec.ts`, D252): a host in Chrome and a guest in Firefox under each netem
profile, shaped on the development container's loopback, for `ORPHEUM_ACCEPTANCE_MINUTES` (30).
It found the follower ignoring a message's transit (fixed: half the shortest round trip, D66), and
that D250 §1's 500 ms left alone cannot meet the 200 ms bound: three-minute runs at 150 ms put home
and mobile in step 100% of the time and congested 94.4%, against 95%. That number, a short run
inside check-pr (it needs `NET_ADMIN` in CI), and a signed-in guest's history (D246 §4 defers it)
are the owner's.
