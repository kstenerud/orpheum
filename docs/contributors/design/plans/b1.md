- **Status:** PROPOSED — phased 2026-11-20 by the agent from [closed-beta.md](closed-beta.md) B1,
  for the owner to confirm. Phase A is built to the assumptions it lists. The questions below are
  the owner's.

What B1 ends with (closed-beta.md, proposed): on a test season with known intro and credits times,
detection finds each within a second, and skip lands there. Watching an episode to its credits counts
down and plays the next, or does not, as the person's setting says. Next Up names the right episode
after watching out of order. A Watch Together group plays on into the next episode together.

Every phase: behaviour changes carry tests that fail when reverted; `make check-pr` passes at its
end.

## Phase A — Next Up on Home

Home has Continue Watching and the newest films and series (`GET /home`), but no Next Up row, the
row play-state.md names as the other most-used surface.

1. **The row** (play-state.md "Continue Watching and Next Up"): for each series the person
   finished an episode of and has no episode partway through, the earliest unwatched episode after
   the latest finished one, in the ordering they watch it in. A series is in exactly one of the two
   rows. Hidden with undo as Continue Watching's rows are (D188), until the series next has
   activity. Selecting it plays at once.
2. **Assumptions the build makes, where play-state.md is silent** (each the conventional choice;
   the owner's to change):
   - the row comes second on Home, after Continue Watching;
   - it is ordered by the person's latest watching in the series, most recent first;
   - it holds at most as many series as Continue Watching's limit (`continueWatchingMaxWorks`);
   - a series last watched long ago stays until it is hidden or finished (no age limit: Jellyfin's
     365-day cutoff is a setting people turn off);
   - series only: a franchise's films (D160's "member, not episode") wait for collections.
3. **Tests:** watching out of order (5 then 3) names 6; an episode partway through moves the series
   to Continue Watching and out of Next Up; the finale finished leaves the row; a new episode added
   after the finale brings the series back; a hide lasts until the next activity.

## Phase B — the segment table and its first detector

media-segments.md is designed (D82, D115, D159, D101) but not built.

1. **The table** (D82): typed segments (`intro`, `recap`, `preview`, `credits`, `commercial`,
   `unknown`, `episode_boundary`) at the Version, each with its provider and confidence. A person's
   correction beats any detector and survives re-detection. A migration appended after the
   checkpoint (rule 8).
2. **The chapter detector**, first and nearly free: chapters the probe already reads, named as an
   intro, a recap or credits ("Intro", "Opening", "OP", "Opening Credits", "Credits", "Ending",
   "ED", "Preview", "Next Episode"), become segments with that provider.
3. **Done when** (media-segments.md): an episode with chapter marks offers a skip with no
   configuration.

## Phase C — skip, and the credits cue

1. **Skip** (D82): while a segment that offers skip plays, a *Skip intro* / *Skip recap* /
   *Skip credits* button over the film; one press seeks to its end; on a TV, focused. A
   `commercial` skips itself.
2. **The credits cue** (D139, D339, up-next.md §9): where an episode has a `credits` segment, the
   Up next offer and its countdown start at it, not at the fixed lead.
3. **Chapters in the player**, if it comes easily (the owner): the chapters as marks on the scrub
   bar and a list to jump to; else the open beta.

## Phase D — audio fingerprints across a season

1. **The job** (D101): the core names a season's files, and the node fingerprints each one's
   opening and closing minutes with FFmpeg's chromaprint muxer, after a stereo downmix (it fails on
   5.1, D115), and reports the spans that repeat across them; the core stores them as `intro` and
   `credits` with the provider and a confidence. The pinned FFmpeg is built with chromaprint, so no
   dependency is added. A background job after a season's scan, at most once per file version.
   *Built, as proposed (2026-11-21):* the node's `findRepeats` command (protocol 67) and the core's
   `find_repeats` job (`internal/core/repeats/repeats.go`): a sweep after each identification pass
   and hourly queues one job for each season, a group of the aired ordering, on each node, with a
   video not measured yet; it names one video of each episode, at most 30, the lowest not measured
   first, and only a season of two episodes or more. The job's target is the season (a `group_id`
   on cache.db's job, since cache schema version 54) and its arguments name the node, so the jobs
   page shows it as the season's, not one episode's (the owner, 2026-11-21); one is open for a
   season at a time, a season on two nodes compared on one and then the other. The node compares
   the opening 10 min and closing 6 min, capped at each video's length. Each video's bytes are measured once per
   measurement version (`repeat_measurement`, step 0095, keyed by payload hash): found, none, or
   failed with FFmpeg's words, never asked again; a video the node refused is left for the next
   sweep. A re-detection replaces only the fingerprint provider's rows, so a person's win.
2. **The acceptance's test season**: generated fixtures with a known intro and credits (a tone
   sequence repeated across "episodes" at known offsets), so detection is held to within a second
   by a test, not by the owner's library.

## Phase E — the host's media keys, and the group episode to episode

1. **The host's media keys and lock screen move the group** (D268 §4): the Media Session's play,
   pause and seek, on the host's device, send the group's command, as the skinned controls do,
   rather than reaching the group as the player's own events. Today seeks are commands
   (`hearMediaSeeks`), and play and pause are the player's (`watchMediaSession`).
2. **The episode-to-episode acceptance:** a Playwright run of two members, the host's episode
   ending, both playing the next together; its device item, a VQ.

**Answered (the owner, 2026-11-27): "Go with your recommendation".** Phase A's assumptions stand
(question 1); question 2 fell away, both detectors being built; a screen to correct a segment waits
for the UI revamp (question 3); no setting to skip intros automatically in B1 (question 4);
question 5 was answered on 2026-11-21. B1 is closed before B2 starts: phase E is checked against
the code and this file brought up to date with what is built.

1. **Phase A's assumptions** (§A.2): are they right? In particular, the row's place (second), and
   no age limit.
2. **Detection's order:** chapters (phase B), then fingerprints (phase D) after the controls
   (phase C), so skip ships on chaptered files first. Or fingerprints before the controls?
3. **A person's correction of a segment** (media-segments.md's "a user correction survives"):
   the table keeps it from the start, but the screen to correct one is UI. Is it in B1, or after
   the UI revamp (B3)? The recommendation is after: the admin can re-run detection meanwhile.
4. **Skip's behaviour:** a button shown while the segment plays (Netflix's), with no automatic
   skip but for commercials. Should a person also get a setting "skip intros automatically" (Plex's)?
   The recommendation is not in B1.
5. **Thumbnails:** the recommendation is one picture every 10 s, 320 px wide, in sprite sheets,
   made by the node in a background pass after the scan, at idle priority, kept in the cache
   (rebuildable, so not backed up). Made for every file, or only once a file is first played?
   The recommendation is every file, at idle priority, since the scrub bar should work on the first
   play.
